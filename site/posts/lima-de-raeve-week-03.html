AI Customer Service for Solo Entrepreneurs: Real Value or FOMO?

Introduction

In my previous Signal posts, I investigated the cost of AI customer service and tested whether AI could correctly classify customer enquiries. This week, I wanted to investigate whether commercial AI customer-service solutions actually deliver the value they promise.

For this analysis, I selected Tidio, a company offering an AI customer-service agent called Lyro. The product promises to automate customer enquiries, reduce response times and allow businesses to operate more efficiently.

But is this a valuable investment for a solo entrepreneur, or simply another example of AI marketing creating FOMO?

Analysing Tidio's Claims

Tidio makes several interesting claims on its website:

Vendor Claim Classification Explanation
Customers average a 75% resolution rate with Lyro. Avoidance The claim provides a specific percentage but doesn't clearly explain the sample size, methodology or applicability to small businesses.
Lyro answers questions using a company's support content. Fact This describes a specific function that can be verified through testing.
Lyro provides accurate and reliable answers. Aspiration Accuracy depends on the available information and the complexity of customer enquiries.
Lyro starts at $32.50 per month. Fact The starting price is published, although usage limits and additional costs must be considered.
Lyro reduces customer-service workload. Aspiration This is a potential benefit, but actual time savings depend on the business and its customer-service volume.

The most interesting claim is the 75% resolution rate. If a business receives 1,000 enquiries monthly, this could theoretically mean 750 enquiries resolved without human intervention.

However, this assumes that the entrepreneur's enquiries resemble those included in Tidio's reported results. A claimed average is not a guaranteed outcome.

Applying the FOMO Framework

1. What specific problem does it solve?

For solo entrepreneurs, customer service can become time-consuming. Automating repetitive questions about deliveries, returns and product information could free up time for other business activities.

2. What happens if the entrepreneur does nothing?

If customer enquiries remain manageable, there may be no immediate need for AI. However, as the business grows, customer service could become increasingly difficult to manage alone.

3. Can the claims be verified?

Tidio offers 50 free Lyro conversations, allowing entrepreneurs to test the system before committing to a subscription. They could measure resolution rates, incorrect responses and time saved.

4. What is the total cost?

Besides the subscription, entrepreneurs must consider setup time, maintaining the knowledge base and monitoring AI responses. The advertised starting price does not necessarily represent the total cost.

5. Is there a simpler solution?

A detailed FAQ page, automated order-tracking emails and predefined response templates might solve many repetitive customer questions without requiring AI.

Final Verdict: PILOT

I would recommend testing Lyro before purchasing a subscription.

Tidio offers potentially valuable functionality, especially for entrepreneurs handling large numbers of repetitive enquiries. However, its advertised performance should not be assumed to apply equally to every business.

For a small business receiving only a few customer questions daily, simpler solutions might be more cost-effective.

The main lesson is that AI should be adopted because it solves a measurable business problem, not because entrepreneurs feel pressured to keep up with technological trends.
