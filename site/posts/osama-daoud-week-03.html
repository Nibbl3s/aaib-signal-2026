Sports Performance: Real Value or FOMO? Deconstructing Zone7's AI Claims

Osama Daoud · 2026-10-11

In my first post I calculated that one injury can cost a top Belgian club around €60,000 in salary alone. So when a company says its AI can warn you before a player gets injured, every club wants to listen. That is exactly why the claims need checking.

The claim

Zone7 is a real company that sells AI to sports clubs. Its system reads the data from players' GPS vests and trackers, such as distance, intensity and rest, and warns the club when a player is at high risk of injury, so the staff can lighten his training. Clubs like Liverpool, Rangers, QPR and Los Angeles FC have worked with it.

Four claims stood out:

  1. "Enable greater durability across your high performance organization through our AI powered insights." (Zone7 homepage)
  2. Clubs using Zone7 "have seen improved player availability compared to prior seasons." (Soccerscene, 2025)
  3. "The teams would have been alerted to a player's increased risk of injury 72.4% of the time that an injury actually occurred", based on 11 teams and 423 injuries from 2019 to 2021. (Zone7's own validation study)
  4. "Liverpool has been utilising Zone7's services since the 2021/22 Premier League season." (Soccerscene, 2025)

The deconstruction

  • Claim 1, "AI powered insights" for "greater durability": Aspiration. It sounds great, but it cannot be measured or proven wrong.
  • Claim 2, clubs "have seen improved player availability": Avoidance. A past result with no clubs named, no numbers and no comparison. Was it the AI, a new coach or luck?
  • Claim 3, alerted for 72.4% of injuries (11 teams, 423 injuries): Fact, but weak. These are real numbers I could check. But Zone7 tested its own product, on old data ("would have been alerted"), the teams are not named and the study says it was not designed to be peer reviewed.
  • Claim 4, Liverpool since 2021/22: Fact. A named club and a named season.

Number 3 looks like the strongest claim, but it is the vendor checking its own homework. It also leaves out the other side: how many times did Zone7 raise an alarm for a player who did not get injured? A club that rests healthy players too often also loses matches.

The FOMO test

What happens if Club Brugge does nothing for 6 months? It keeps working as it does now: the staff look at the GPS data themselves. That is more work and slower, but nothing breaks. So there is no urgent reason to buy, unless the club can show that injuries are costing it a lot of money right now.

Can the claims be verified? Not from the website. The one real study was done by Zone7 itself. A club would need to test it on its own players.

Is there a simpler solution? Pro clubs already have GPS vests, physios and sports scientists. Using that data better, with simple rules like "if a player runs much more than usual this week, give him a lighter session", could solve a big part of the problem before paying for an AI.

The verdict

Pilot, not buy. Zone7 is real AI working on a real problem, and the Liverpool link shows serious clubs take it seriously. But the best evidence comes from Zone7 itself. Club Brugge should run it next to its own staff for one season on its own players, and compare: did it warn about injuries the staff missed, and how many false alarms did it give? Only if it beats the staff should the club pay for it.
