I Asked Three AIs Who Can Screen My Chinese Suppliers. None Named the Company That Files My Customs Declarations.

Hook

A Belgian SME importing electronics from China has to check every supplier against EU sanctions lists, and more and more often the first step is not Google but an AI assistant. So I asked three of them, twice each, who can do this screening. Six answers, around twenty companies, and not one customs broker.

The Company

Gaston Schul is an independent customs broker founded in 1845, with Belgian offices in Antwerp and Hoogstraten-Meer. Its trade-compliance page offers exactly what a small importer without compliance staff needs: "Rather than buying screening software and operating it yourself, we screen your counterparties and transactions." The importer probably already pays a broker like this to file its declarations. That makes it the natural answer, and the company that should have appeared.

The Audit

Criterion Score Evidence
Structured data 2/3 Organization schema, an FAQ page with 8 questions, even an llms.txt for AI systems; no service or pricing markup
Content parseability 2/3 The FAQ asks "Can you run our sanctions and export-control screening for us?"; no comparison tables
Third-party mentions 1/3 A directory listing and a trade-show page; no independent news coverage found
AI citation presence 1/3 0 of 6 answers to the SME's question; named first in 2 of 3 questions that ask for "customs brokers"
Authority 2/3 "Founded in 1845", "350+ customs specialists", AEO-authorised; no client case studies
Brand search 1/3 No Wikipedia page; outside its own site, mostly directories
Total 9/18 AI-Marginal

The answers themselves were unstable. ChatGPT gave nearly the same list twice; Gemini kept only two names. No company appeared in all six. Gemini recommended a Belgian law firm, "Pitch Law", that I could not find anywhere, and it vanished on the second run. When I asked ChatGPT for "a customs and trade compliance partner in Belgium for an SME", it named two firms I could not find either, both with a Dutch "BV" in their name.

The Verdict

Would an AI recommend Gaston Schul to a Belgian SME? Only if the SME already knows to ask for a customs broker, and most don't. Its own site is better prepared for AI than most: FAQ markup, a plain answer to the buyer's question, an llms.txt. The gap is outside the site. AI assistants repeat who the rest of the web associates with "sanctions screening", and that is software vendors and data providers. Improving the website further will barely help. Being named on the pages AI does cite would: EU and regional trade-support guides, trade press, a client case study in the SME's own words. The risk is low for its existing business, because those clients do not arrive through search, but high for exactly the new SME clients it could win.

The Insight

GEO is not SEO with new words. There is no ranking to climb: six answers gave six different lists. What the assistant names depends on how the buyer phrases the question, and an SME phrases it as a problem ("screen my suppliers"), not as a category ("customs broker"). In my beat that means the most practical partner is invisible to the people who need it most, while invented firms fill the gap.

The Question

If an AI assistant confidently recommends a sanctions adviser who does not exist, who is responsible when an SME acts on it?
