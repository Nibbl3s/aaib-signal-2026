Would it be better to just let it be?

week: 2

title: Is AI the right tool?

author: Luna Van der Heyden

beat: “Algorithmic Homogenization: The Operational and Brand Costs of AI-Driven Editing in Commercial Publishing”

skill: Capablility skepticism

date: 2026-02-10

Nowadays in commercial publishing, everyone would use anything to minimise the overloaded tasks, like speeding up manuscript reviews. AI would be everyone's hero in streamlining the headache of checking every single word for spelling mistakes or typos.

But some of us have fallen into the trap of efficiency and the perfection that AI gives, accidentally fixing things again and again, smoothing sentences until the human soul is gone. The meaning and emotional feeling of that moment when the writer decided to write that sentence vanishes entirely.

So, what is the meaning of human connection through letters? What is the art in it?

When AI suffers from algorithmic homogenization, it acts like a crazy perfectionist, forcing writers to doubt their own authentic style. Fixing and adjusting is like a never-ending story.

Why? Because of the myth of the “correct sentence”. If an AI hallucinates a historical date, a researcher can check a factbook. But if an AI alters a poetic, jagged sentence into a smooth one, there is no master text to prove it wrong. The AI operates on a statistical average, predicting the most common, frictionless way to arrange words. When it presents its "smoothed" version, it frames human art as something to be fixed.

Furthermore, there is the cost of losing the author’s voice. A book’s unique selling point is the author’s distinct perspective. Who would buy the same old story? When AI removes unconventional voices that make literature marketable and turns books into uniform commodities, it breaks the author-reader bond and lowers long-term sales.

Therefore, what should we do now—destroy AI entirely or go back in time as it was? No.

The simplest solution would be separating mechanics from style: using standard spell-checkers, custom grammar rules, or traditional style guides for objective errors, and relying purely on human critique groups, beta readers, and human editors for voice. All of these help! But the most important thing is to use your brain and your heart. We are just so stuck in this perfection loop that we forgot to embrace the imperfection that gives life its reason: to think, to reread, to get frustrated over small mistakes, or even to joke about them. These are part of being alive. Sometimes, just let it be; we don't need perfection.

In conclusion, just because an AI can rewrite a sentence doesn't mean it should. In creative publishing, the cost of losing the human voice is far higher than the time saved by a machine.
