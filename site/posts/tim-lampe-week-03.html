AI in M&A Valuation: Real Value or FOMO? Deconstructing Valutico's AI Claims

Tim Lampe · 2026-10-05

AI in M&A Valuation: Real Value or FOMO? Deconstructing Valutico's AI Claims

In M&A valuation, the comparable-company set decides the price: wrong comps, wrong number. Vendors now promise that AI will pick them for you, so separating a tested claim from a hopeful one decides whether a firm buys a tool or just a pitch.

The Claim

Valutico is a Vienna-based valuation platform for advisory, audit, banking and private equity teams, combining a deal database, AI-assisted peer selection and automatic reports. Three claims on its public pages stand out. On peers, it "automatically suggests the most relevant comps." On speed, valuations are "finished in half the time, with twice the confidence." From a customer: "it pays out 10 times over." What is missing: any published price, only "Book Your Demo", and any accuracy figure for the suggested comps. I used valutico.com as of 5 October 2026 and did not test the product.

The Deconstruction

# Claim Tag Reasoning
1 "Trusted by 850+ teams" Avoidance No names, no definition of "trusted"; other pages cite lower counts.
2 1.1M+ deals, 53K+ companies Fact Specific; checkable in a trial, including sector coverage.
3 "Suggests the most relevant comps" Aspiration "Relevant" is undefined; no accuracy figure.
4 "Half the time, twice the confidence" Avoidance No baseline; confidence is unmeasurable.
5 "Pays out 10 times over" Avoidance Testimonial with no baseline or method.
6 "Client-ready reports in seconds" Fact Timeable in a demo; "client-ready" depends on editing needed.
7 Price: "Book Your Demo" Avoidance Withholds the first number a buyer needs.

Two Facts, one Aspiration, four Avoidances. The testable claims (2, 6) concern data volume and report speed. The claim about AI judgment (3), the one that matters for comps, cannot be tested from the page, and the evidence of value (1, 4, 5) is testimonial. Row 4 also conflicts with its own page, which quotes a customer saving "a third" of the time, not half. Valutico is not hollow, but its strongest-sounding claims are its least checkable.

The FOMO Test

Q3, can we verify? Partly. Data coverage and report speed can be tested in a demo, and a buyer can re-run a past valuation, where the answer is known, and compare comps and hours. The AI-comps claim and the 10x ROI cannot be verified from vendor material; ask for three named reference customers.

Q4, total cost? Unanswerable. No price is shown and modules are sold separately. Ask for a written quote covering licence, modules, onboarding and exit terms.

Q5, simpler solution? Possibly. Excel templates, a cheaper data source and a report template may cover most of a standard valuation. If they cover 80% for 20% of the cost, the platform must prove the rest, mainly in comps quality.

The Verdict

Real product, unproven AI claims. Valutico is not a chatbot on a rules engine: its data and reports are testable. But AI-picked comps, the claim that matters most, has no definition of "relevant" and no accuracy figure, and its proof is testimonials without baselines. Buyers should pilot, not buy or walk away: re-run one past valuation, compare time and comps with your own, and get the price in writing. If the comps hold up and total cost beats Excel plus a data source, buy. If the vendor refuses a trial on your data, walk away.
