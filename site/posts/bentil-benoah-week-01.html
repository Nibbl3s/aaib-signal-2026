What Does AI Actually Cost?

Bentil Benoah Boakye · 2026-10-02

What Does AI Actually Cost?

Before this week, I knew that companies had to pay to use AI, but I did not really know how the price was calculated. I learned that one of the main things you pay for is tokens. Tokens are pieces of text that the AI processes, and they are not exactly the same as words.

For my beat, AI in ERP and Business Process Automation, I looked at a simple example. Imagine a company using an ERP system like Odoo and receiving 30,000 customer emails every month. AI could be used to answer questions about orders, invoices and returns.

If every email has around 200 input tokens and the AI gives an answer of around 400 output tokens, the monthly usage would be:

  • Input: 30,000 × 200 = 6,000,000 tokens
  • Output: 30,000 × 400 = 12,000,000 tokens

If we use the Standard vendor price from the exercise, the input price is $0.03 per 1,000 tokens and the output price is $0.06 per 1,000 tokens.

So:

  • Input cost = $180
  • Output cost = $720
  • Total token cost = $900 per month

Using the course exchange rate of $1 = €0.92, this is about €828 per month.

At first I thought this was actually quite cheap for 30,000 emails. But then we also had to calculate the cost when the AI makes mistakes.

The Standard system has an 85% success rate, so 15% of the emails would still need a human to fix them. That is 4,500 emails. If every mistake costs €5 to fix, the human cost becomes €22,500 per month.

This means the real monthly cost is around €23,328, and not just €828.

This was the most interesting part for me. The tokens themselves can be cheap, but that does not mean the whole AI system is cheap. If the AI makes many mistakes, the company still needs people to correct them.

I think this is important for ERP systems like Odoo because they deal with real business information such as orders, invoices and customer data. If AI makes a mistake there, it can create more work instead of saving time.

I also learned that different languages can use different amounts of tokens. This is important for companies in Belgium because customers can communicate in Dutch, French and English.

My main takeaway is that when a company wants to use AI in an ERP system, it should not only look at the AI price. It should also look at how accurate it is and how much the mistakes will cost.
