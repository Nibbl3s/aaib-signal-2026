AI in HR: Real Value or FOMO? Deconstructing Carv's AI Claims
Falling for AI hype in HR tech isn't just a waste of budget, it's a massive compliance risk. Under the EU AI Act, recruitment algorithms are flagged as high-risk, meaning any bias or screening mistakes fall directly on the employer, not the software vendor. Because of this, learning to separate actual useful tools from pure marketing fluff is something every HR professional needs to do before buying into a new platform.
Carv is an AI recruitment platform aimed at high-volume hiring. They sell their tool as an "agentic platform" that takes over the whole recruitment pipeline to get rid of admin work. Looking at their homepage, a few big claims immediately stand out: "Carv’s AI agents autonomously handle the busywork, so your team can operate at 10x capacity," "From 'apply' to 'hire' Carv's agentic platform is purpose-built to transform your volume hiring process," and "Carefree volume hiring that completely eliminates hiring friction and human error." Basically, they are trying to convince buyers that their AI agents can act as fully autonomous recruiters with zero friction.
Analyzing these claims using the Fact/Aspiration/Avoidance framework shows what's actually under the hood. Saying the platform connects candidates and automates admin steps from apply to hire counts as a Fact, because parsing CVs and triggering ATS updates via APIs are basic, verifiable software tasks. On the other hand, promising that a team will operate at "10x capacity" is clearly an Aspiration. That kind of multiplier might happen in a perfect best-case scenario, but real-world gains depend on workflow setups and still need recruiters checking the output. The worst claim is that the tool "completely eliminates hiring friction and human error," which falls under Avoidance. It’s super misleading because no tool eliminates human error, and generative AI can easily hallucinate or misinterpret a candidate's background.
Running Carv through the FOMO test helps cut through the sales pitch. On the positive side, when asking what problem they are actually solving, Carv targets a real headache slow response times and huge admin loads in volume hiring instead of just talking about generic "AI innovation." But when it comes to verifying their claims, flashy website demos aren't enough; you'd need a real pilot project to see if that "10x capacity" boost is real. Lastly, looking at whether a simpler solution exists reveals that basic ATS rules, email templates, and calendar links can easily fix 80% of hiring delays without needing complex AI agents.
Verdict: Run a limited pilot. Carv definitely brings real value as a workflow tool that speeds up repetitive tasks like interview scheduling and data entry. However, claims about "10x capacity" and "zero human error" are just marketing wrapped around standard LLM APIs. HR teams shouldn't walk away completely, but they definitely shouldn't buy a full license right away either. A small pilot focused only on admin tasks while keeping human oversight in place is the safest way to go.
