Insurance Claims: Real Value or FOMO? Deconstructing Tractable's AI Claims

Guillaume Vandenweghe · 2026-10-09

In May 2026 the Luxembourg insurer Foyer announced that Tractable's AI will assess car damage from photos. It looks like the obvious next step for a Benelux claims department. Would Tractable's pitch survive the same test I put my own Build through?

The claim

Tractable sells computer vision that looks at photos of a damaged car and produces a damage assessment and repair estimate. Its auto-claims page leads with three big numbers: "3 mins", "10x" and "70%", the last one next to "Increase your chance of straight-through processing". The homepage adds "ultra-precise damage assessments by analyzing images down to the pixel", "processes thousands of claims daily" and "With every deployment, our AI learns and improves."

Foyer's press release (6 May 2026) is more modest. The AI is used for "low-severity losses", it "accelerates the processing of straightforward claims" and it "reduces the need for garage visits". Exactly the pitch a Belgian claims director would hear.

The deconstruction

Claim Tag Why, and the question it dodges
"3 mins", "10x" Avoidance No label at all. Three minutes for what? Ten times faster than which baseline?
"70%" chance of straight-through processing Avoidance 70% of which claims, at which insurer, measured by whom?
"ultra-precise … down to the pixel" Aspiration Pixel precision is not accuracy in euros. How far is the estimate from the expert's?
"processes thousands of claims daily" Fact Checkable with one reference call, but it counts volume, not correct answers.
"our AI learns and improves" Aspiration Learns from whose data, and who checks that it improves instead of drifting?
Foyer: "low-severity losses" Fact A specific, checkable scope.
Foyer: "reduces the need for garage visits" Aspiration A benefit with no number yet.

The bigger problem is what the pitch leaves out. No error rate, no price, no figure for how often an estimate is corrected later, and no answer to who carries the cost when the estimate is too low for the customer. That last one matters in my beat: a low estimate becomes a complaint, and a complaint can become an Ombudsman case.

Still, the technology itself is real. Reading damage from images is a mature computer-vision task, the same type as the defect cameras FlandersTextiles turned down. The hype is in the numbers, not in the AI.

The FOMO test

  1. Specific problem? Yes. Small motor claims wait days for an expert or garage visit.
  2. Nothing for six months? Claims still get paid, only slower. No crisis, so no reason to sign a long contract now.
  3. Verify independently? Yes, and this is Tractable's strongest point: run it in shadow mode next to human experts on our own claims and compare the euros.
  4. Total cost? Unknown. No public price, plus integration with the claims system, a photo app, expert time for flagged cases and agreements with garages.
  5. Simpler 80% solution? A human expert who assesses photos remotely, plus a rule that fast-tracks claims under a fixed amount.

The verdict

Pilot. Not buy: the headline numbers are unsourced and the price is missing, so I cannot write the business case. Not walk away either: unlike GhentBakery's forecast, this is mature technology on a checkable task, and refusing it would repeat FlandersTextiles' mistake.

The pilot: low-severity motor claims only, 90 days in shadow mode, success criteria written down before the start, and an exit clause.

The one question I would send Tractable: "On low-severity motor claims in a European portfolio, in what share of cases did your estimate differ by more than 10% from the amount finally paid, and who measured it?"
