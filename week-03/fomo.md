\# Week 3 — FOMO Framework



\## NexusAI evaluation



\### 1. What specific business problem will this solve?



NexusAI claims to optimize supply chains through demand forecasting, routing and warehouse optimization. The proposal therefore identifies a plausible business problem, but it does not quantify EuroLogistics' current problem.



Before buying, I would want to know the current cost of inaccurate forecasts, late deliveries, fuel consumption and warehouse picking time. Without those baselines, the claimed improvements cannot be translated into actual business value.



\*\*Assessment: Weakly supported.\*\*



\---



\### 2. What happens if we do nothing for 6 months?



The proposal does not quantify the cost of doing nothing. Instead, it creates urgency by saying that only two pilot slots remain.



That is not the same as showing that delaying the decision will cause a meaningful business loss. EuroLogistics should calculate the expected cost of its current problems over six months and compare that with the expected value of NexusAI.



\*\*Assessment: FOMO signal.\*\*



\---



\### 3. Can we verify the vendor's claims independently?



Verification is currently weak.



NexusAI mentions 200+ European customers, internal benchmarking and client self-reporting, but does not provide enough independent evidence. The 94% accuracy claim is particularly difficult to evaluate because the proposal does not explain what “accuracy” means or how it was measured.



The appropriate test would be a controlled pilot using EuroLogistics' own historical and current data, with success criteria agreed before the pilot starts.



\*\*Assessment: Not independently verified.\*\*



\---



\### 4. What is the total cost, not just the license?



The proposal gives an annual license price of €252,000, but this is unlikely to represent the entire cost of ownership.



EuroLogistics would also need to consider integration, internal staff time, data preparation, implementation, training, support, possible API or LLM costs, maintenance and the cost of switching away later.



The money-back guarantee also needs to be examined carefully: the contract should define exactly what counts as “improvement”.



\*\*Assessment: Incomplete.\*\*



\---



\### 5. Is there a simpler, cheaper solution that solves 80% of the problem?



Potentially yes.



Some parts of the proposed system, such as basic forecasting, routing rules and warehouse performance tracking, may be achievable through existing software, spreadsheets or more conventional analytics.



However, if NexusAI can genuinely combine large-scale forecasting and optimization across multiple data sources, AI could provide additional value. The key question is whether that additional value justifies the cost and complexity.



\*\*Assessment: Needs comparison with simpler alternatives.\*\*



\---



\## Verdict: PILOT



I would \*\*not buy NexusAI immediately\*\*, but I also would not walk away completely.



The underlying problems are real and AI could potentially help with them. However, the proposal relies too heavily on vague benefits, unsupported percentages, internal benchmarking and urgency.



A controlled pilot is the sensible middle ground. The pilot should use EuroLogistics' own data and have pre-agreed metrics for forecasting accuracy, fuel costs, delivery performance and picking time. The contract should also specify the total cost and exactly how the money-back guarantee works.



If NexusAI cannot demonstrate measurable improvement against an agreed baseline, EuroLogistics should walk away.
