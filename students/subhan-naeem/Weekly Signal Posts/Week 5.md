# Can AI Find ML6? A GEO Audit of Ghent’s “AI Engineering Powerhouse”

## Hook

When a Belgian CFO asks an AI assistant to recommend an AI consultancy, the answer names a selection of firms, and everyone else risks never entering the conversation. In my beat, AI in business consulting, that shortlist is now where deals begin. So I audited one company to see whether it makes the cut.

## The Company

ML6 is a Ghent-based AI engineering company founded in 2013, with offices in four other European countries. It sells AI advisory, AI engineering and its new Unum agent platform to clients such as P&G, Pfizer and ING. I chose it because it was the only firm named in both runs of my live ChatGPT query for Belgian AI consultancies, making it a useful test of how visible a specialist really is.

## The Audit

I scored ML6 on six criteria (0–3) and got **12/18**.

- **Structured data, 1/3:** The only JSON-LD on the homepage is `SiteNavigationElement`, which maps the menu. There’s no `Organization`, `Service` or FAQ markup, so structured data provides limited information about ML6’s identity and services.
- **Content parseability, 2/3:** Navigation is clear, but heading levels jump from h1 to h5 and h3, descriptive copy sits in h5 tags, and there’s no FAQ or comparison table.
- **Third-party mentions, 2/3:** Google page one shows LinkedIn, OpenAI’s partner page, VAIA and a Google Business Profile. These are mostly profiles, not independent editorial coverage.
- **AI citation, 2/3:** Asked for the best AI consulting providers, ChatGPT ranked ML6 sixth of seven as a “strong Belgian specialist”, behind Accenture, McKinsey, IBM, BCG X and Deloitte. In my earlier two-run Belgium query, it was the only firm named both times.
- **Authority signals, 3/3:** Named leadership, Mediahuis and Scout24 cases, original benchmarks on 15,728 emails, and OpenAI, AWS and Azure partner status.
- **Brand search, 2/3:** ml6.eu ranks first with sitelinks and a business panel rated 4.8 from 28 reviews, but there’s no Wikipedia page or full knowledge panel.

ML6 is strong on substance but weaker on machine-readable labelling.

## The Verdict

ML6 scored 12/18, or **AI-Present**. An AI agent asked for a Belgian AI engineering partner would likely mention it: it surfaced in my targeted queries and in a generic one. It would rarely lead the answer, though, and my live query returned different shortlists on each run, so placement is not guaranteed. Its biggest gaps are third-party mention density and machine-readable structure. Global consultancies with more independent coverage may therefore rank above it.

## The Insight

ChatGPT said its picks were informed by “Belgian AI consultancy directories”, not company homepages. That’s the lesson for my beat: AI consultancies sell AI, yet many haven’t made themselves machine-readable. Discoverability can depend on reviews, directories and press, alongside on-site improvements such as schema markup. Firms that improve these signals may have a better chance of appearing on a buyer’s shortlist before the buyer ever visits a website.

Would adding structured data make ML6 more likely to appear in AI recommendations, or does third-party coverage matter more?
