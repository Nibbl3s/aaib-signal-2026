Can AI Find ML6? A GEO Audit of Ghent’s “AI Engineering Powerhouse”
Hook
When a Belgian CFO asks an AI assistant to recommend an AI consultancy, the answer names a sufficient of firms, and everyone else never enters the conversation. In my beat, AI in business consulting, that shortlist is now where deals begin. So I audited one company to see whether it makes the cut.
The Company
ML6 is a Ghent-based AI engineering company founded in 2013, with offices in 4 more European countries headquartered in Ghent. It sells AI advisory, AI engineering and its new Unum agent platform to clients such as P&G, Pfizer and ING. I chose it because it was the only firm named in both runs of my live ChatGPT query for Belgian AI consultancies, which makes it a fair test of how visible a specialist really is.
The Audit
I scored ML6 on six criteria (0–3) and got 12/18.
•	Structured data, 1/3: The only JSON-LD on the homepage is SiteNavigationElement, which maps the menu. There’s no Organization, Service or FAQ markup, so a machine can’t confirm what ML6 is.
•	Content Parseability, 2/3: Navigation is clear, but heading levels jump from h1 to h5 and h3, descriptive copy sits in h5 tags, and there’s no FAQ or comparison table.
•	Third-party mentions, 2/3: Google page one shows LinkedIn, OpenAI’s partner page, VAIA and a Google Business Profile. These are mostly profiles, not independent editorial coverage.
•	AI citation, 2/3: Asked for the best AI consulting providers, ChatGPT ranked ML6 sixth of seven as a “strong Belgian specialist”, behind Accenture, McKinsey, IBM, BCG X and Deloitte. In my earlier two-run Belgium query, it was the only firm named both times.
•	Authority signals, 3/3: Named leadership, Mediahuis and Scout24 cases, original benchmarks on 15,728 emails, and OpenAI, AWS and Azure partner status.
•	Brand search, 2/3: ml6.eu ranks first with sitelinks and a business panel rated 4.8 from 28 reviews, but there’s no Wikipedia page or full knowledge panel.
ML6 is strong on substance and weak on machine-readable labelling.
The Verdict
ML6 scored 12/18, AI-Present. An AI agent asked for a Belgian AI engineering partner would likely mention it: it surfaced in my targeted queries and in a generic one. It would rarely lead the answer, though, and my live query returned different shortlists on each run, so any placement is probabilistic. Its biggest gaps are third-party mention density and machine readable structure. An AI sees mostly directory entries and a menu-only schema, so global consultancies with far more independent coverage rank above of it.
The Insight
ChatGPT said its picks were informed by “Belgian AI consultancy directories”, not company homepages. That’s the lesson for my beat: AI consultancies sell AI, yet many haven’t made themselves machine readable. Discoverability is built mostly off-site, through reviews, directories and press, with cheap on-site fixes like schema markup alongside. Firms that do this get their spot early on the the shortlist before buyers ever visit a website.
The Question
Would adding structured data make ML6 more likely to appear in AI recommendations, or does third-party coverage matter more?
