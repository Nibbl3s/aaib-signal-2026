# AI in Business Consulting: The Cost of AI

## Hook

**How much does AI actually cost a consulting firm?**

When we hear about consulting firms adopting AI, we often focus on what AI can do: analyse information, research markets, summarise documents and help consultants prepare recommendations. But there is another question that is easy to overlook: **what does all of this actually cost?**

Since my beat is **AI in Business Consulting**, I wanted to use an international consulting project as a simple example and see how much an AI research assistant could cost.

## The Numbers

Imagine a consulting team working on an international market-entry project. They use an AI assistant to analyse documents, summarise research and prepare first drafts for consultants.

For this example, I assume the AI processes **5,000 research tasks per month**.

* **Input per task:** 1,000 tokens
* **Output per task:** 500 tokens
* **Monthly input:** 5,000,000 tokens
* **Monthly output:** 2,500,000 tokens

Using a hypothetical API price of **$0.03 per 1,000 input tokens** and **$0.06 per 1,000 output tokens**:

* **Input cost:** $150
* **Output cost:** $150
* **Total AI cost:** $300 per month

Using the course exchange rate of **$1 = €0.92**, that is approximately **€276 per month**.

This is only the AI usage cost. It does not include the cost of the consultants who review the output or other software and implementation costs.

## The Insight

What I found interesting from this week's activities is that **the cheapest AI option is not necessarily the cheapest business option**.

In the EuroShop exercise, the Budget AI had the lowest token cost, but its higher error rate created much larger correction costs. This made me think about consulting differently. A consulting firm could save money by choosing a cheaper AI model, but if that model produces unreliable market research, consultants have to spend more time checking and correcting the results, which would eventually increase the amount spent on a project.

For consulting, reliability is especially important because AI output can eventually influence recommendations given to clients. A small saving on API costs may not be worthwhile if the quality of the work decreases.

My main takeaway is that consulting firms should not only ask:

> **“How much does the AI cost?”**

They should also ask:

> **“How reliable is it, and how much does it cost when it gets something wrong?”**

## The Question

**If AI usage itself becomes very cheap, will reliability and human verification become more important than the actual cost of the AI?**
