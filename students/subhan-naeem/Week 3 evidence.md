### Top 5 Avoidances in the NexusAI Proposal

| # | What the proposal avoids | Why it matters | Question I'd ask NexusAI |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Internal benchmarking:** The 94% forecast accuracy is mentioned, but the testing methodology is not explained. | "Up to 94%" from the vendor's own tests says nothing about the accuracy a client would actually get. | "What was the average accuracy on a held-out test set, and can an independent party verify it?" |
| 2 | **Client-reported results:** The results come from clients themselves, with no independent verification. | Clients who are unhappy may not report, and clients who want to justify the purchase may over-report. | "Can I speak to three named clients and see before-and-after data?" |
| 3 | **Unspecified LLM technology:** The proposal says it uses "leading LLM technology" but doesn't say which model. | The model affects quality, cost and data privacy, so the buyer can't judge the platform without knowing it. | "Which LLM do you use, who hosts it, and who pays for the usage?" |
| 4 | **Limited pilot availability:** Only two pilot slots are said to remain, with no evidence that this is true. | It creates pressure to decide quickly and skip proper due diligence. | "When does the next pilot slot open, and why is the offer limited?" |
| 5 | **Missing costs beyond the €252,000:** The proposal quotes Year 1 pricing but doesn't say who pays for LLM usage, model retraining, support after onboarding, or switching costs. | The real total cost is higher than the quoted figure, so the buyer can't compare it with the expected benefits. | "What is the full cost over three years, including everything not listed?" |

## 2. Applying the FOMO Framework

| **Question** | **Answer** |
|---|---|
| **1. What business problem does it solve?** | NexusAI helps companies with forecasting, delivery routes and warehouse work. It could save time and money, but the company needs to know which problem it actually wants to solve. |
| **2. Is there a real reason to buy it now?** | Not necessarily. The claim that only two pilot slots are left could pressure companies to decide quickly. They should first check if they really need the software. |
| **3. Can the results be verified?** | Not completely. The company makes claims about accuracy and cost savings, but some results come from internal tests and customer reports without much detail or verifiable data. |
| **4. Is the price worth the benefits?** | It is difficult to say without knowing the full price, including implementation, maintenance and AI usage costs. |
| **5. Are there simpler alternatives?** | Yes. The company could improve its existing forecasting tools or warehouse processes first, before paying for a new AI system. |

I think I would run a small pilot rather than buy the full system immediately. NexusAI might be useful, but the company needs proof of its results and total costs first.

## 3. GhentBakery vs. FlandersTextiles

| **Dimension** | **GhentBakery (Bought too much)** | **FlandersTextiles (Missed real value)** |
|---|---|---|
| **What did they evaluate?** | They focused on the vendor's promises about AI and 92% accuracy instead of checking how much better it was than their current system, which was already at 89%. | They focused on the risks of AI and ignored the potential savings from detecting more fabric defects. |
| **Did they use the FOMO framework?** | No. They didn't properly check the business problem. | They rejected AI without properly checking the benefits and costs. |
| **Did they assess modality fit (Week 2)?** | No. They didn't check whether the AI features were suitable or whether they needed them. Their existing spreadsheet was already very effective. | No. They treated all AI as the same. Computer vision is suitable for identifying patterns and defects in fabric images. |
| **Did they calculate total cost (Week 1)?** | No. They overlooked the full cost, including the subscription, implementation, staff training and consultant fees. | Not properly. They focused on the €80,000 implementation and €3,000 monthly cost without comparing them with the potential savings they could have had. |
| **What was the root cause of the mistake?** | They trusted the vendor's promises and bought the product because it sounded innovative and modern. | They were too scared about AI because of their reputation and rejected a potentially useful solution based on general fears rather than evidence. |
| **What question should they have asked first?** | “What specific problem are we trying to solve, and how much better is this system than our current method?” | “How much are missed defects costing us, and can we verify that AI will reduce these losses?” |

Both companies made mistakes because they did not properly evaluate the vendor's claims. GhentBakery trusted the promises too easily, while FlandersTextiles rejected AI without even checking much about it. Businesses should compare the expected benefits, total costs and actual performance before making a decision.
