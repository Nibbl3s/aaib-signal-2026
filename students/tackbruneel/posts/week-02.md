**Hook:** Last week I worked out that an AI quote bot would cost me €0.03 a month in tokens and about €150 a month in wrong prices. This week I asked the earlier question: should AI be setting my prices at all? I ran the five-question framework on my own quotes, and the answer is no. The reason surprised me more than the verdict.

**The Numbers:** The use case is a client sending an inquiry and AI drafting the quote, including the price. Here are the five questions, answered with my own data.

1. **Do I already know the answer?** Yes. My sent mails show €60/h for a private 18th birthday, €80/h for a youth-movement party and €500–700 as the indication for a wedding. A price is hours × rate + extras: a formula, not a judgment.
2. **Is error cost higher than slowness cost?** Clearly. A wrong price costs me about €150 (my Week 1 estimate). A slow price costs almost nothing: my last two quote replies went out the same evening and the next morning, and nobody complained.
3. **Is the information stable?** Per season, yes. But it's not written down anywhere. Nothing says when €60 applies and when €80 does. An AI would have to guess, and guessing is exactly what produces confident wrong prices.
4. **Can I verify the output?** Yes, but only by recalculating it, which removes the time saved.
5. **What's the simplest tool?** A price table: event type → rate, plus km × travel rate, plus optional sound & light from my partner. Accountable, which I already use for invoices, can store these as fixed quote lines.

Four out of five answers point away from AI. The trade-off in money terms: a price table costs about one evening to set up and €0 a month to run, with an error rate of zero as long as the inputs are right. An AI quote costs €0.03 a month plus an error rate I can't predict, on the one number the client holds me to.

**The Insight:** The part that does need help isn't the price, it's the inputs. This week I built a small tool that reads my real booking inquiries (10 form answers and 5 e-mails) and extracts the date, times, venue, guest count and whether I need to bring my own gear. I ran each of the 15 inputs twice. It got **50%** fully right. Dates were 30/30 correct. But on one graduation party it said the decks were on site in both runs, while the client had written that there was only a mixer and asked me to bring back-up. A price table would happily calculate a perfect quote for a gig where I show up without decks.

So the division of labour is: **AI may read the messy message, rules do the money, and I check what goes in between.** Skepticism here isn't "AI is bad". It's being exact about which step can afford a mistake. The price can't, and it doesn't need AI anyway. The reading step can afford mistakes only if I catch them, and at 50% I still have to.

**The Question:** If writing down my pricing rules is what makes AI unnecessary for pricing, how many "AI use cases" in small businesses are really just rules nobody has written down yet?
