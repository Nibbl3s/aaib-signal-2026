# Trade Compliance: Real Value or FOMO? Deconstructing ComplyAdvantage's AI Claims

**Hook**

In Week 1 I emailed ComplyAdvantage asking what their sanctions screening platform would cost a mid sized Belgian importer. They never replied. This week I went back to their public marketing to see what they are actually claiming, since a vendor who will not quote you a price will still quite happily tell you their product is market leading. In a field where a missed sanctions match can mean a criminal referral, the gap between what a compliance vendor promises and what they can prove is not a minor annoyance, it is the whole risk.

**The Claim**

ComplyAdvantage sells AI powered sanctions, watchlist, and adverse media screening to financial institutions and regulated businesses, positioning itself as a modern, real time alternative to legacy compliance systems. Three claims from their own site: their Watchlists and Sanctions Screening customers include ABInBev, AJ Bell, Zoopla, and Plaid, AJ Bell achieved an 82 percent reduction in alert volumes using the platform, and the company can automate 95 percent of reviews and cut onboarding times by 50 percent.

**The Deconstruction**

| # | Claim | Tag | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Named customers: ABInBev, AJ Bell, Zoopla, Plaid | Fact | Named, checkable companies, unlike NexusAI's unnamed "200+ companies" |
| 2 | AJ Bell, 82% reduction in alert volumes | Fact, weaker than it looks | Named client, specific figure, but no stated period or baseline volume |
| 3 | Automate 95% of reviews, cut onboarding by 50% | Avoidance | Present tense, no client named, no denominator, no study cited |
| 4 | Identify sanctioned individuals before official notifications | Aspiration | A forward looking capability claim with no case example or test attached |
| 5 | Tens of thousands of global data sources | Avoidance | Vague quantifier standing in for an actual number, no sources named |
| 6 | Mapping to EU and FATF crimes is market leading | Avoidance | Unfalsifiable superlative, no comparison offered |

Two of six claims here are genuinely strong, named clients and a specific, attributable result. That is a meaningfully better ratio than the fictional NexusAI proposal, where ten of sixteen claims were avoidance. Real vendors are not uniformly hollow, they mix strong evidence with vague marketing in the same paragraph, and the skill is separating one from the other rather than distrusting the whole page.

**The FOMO Test**

Question 3, can the claims be verified independently, is the most revealing one here. The AJ Bell result is attributable and could be checked directly with AJ Bell, which is a real strength most vendor claims lack entirely. But claim 3, the 95 percent automation figure, has no client attached at all, so it cannot be checked the same way, and a buyer should demand to know whose reviews, over what period, before treating it as anything more than marketing. Question 4, what is the total cost beyond the license, is also sharpened by my own Week 1 finding, ComplyAdvantage would not even confirm a self serve number for a real, described scenario, which means total cost for a business my size is currently unknowable from outside the sales process entirely.

**The Verdict**

Pilot, not buy, and only after the vendor answers what my Week 1 outreach could not. The named client evidence is real and worth taking seriously, unlike NexusAI's entirely unnamed base. But the unattributed efficiency figures need a source before they inform any decision, and the complete silence on pricing at my described volume means total cost of ownership is currently a blank page, not just an incomplete one. A buyer should ask specifically for the AJ Bell case study in full, ask which reviews the 95 percent figure covers, and treat the earlier unanswered pricing email as diagnostic in itself, a vendor unwilling to give a self serve number is a vendor asking you to commit before you can calculate what you are committing to.
