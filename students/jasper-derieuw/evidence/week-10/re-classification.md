# Ten Scenarios: My Gut, Then the Framework

**How this was done:** I wrote my gut classification of the ten course scenarios on 16 November 2026, ahead of the class on 7 December, without looking anything up and before opening the answer key. Honesty note: I had already classified my own tool under the AI Act (Week 10), so my gut was not blank; I had not seen these scenarios or their answers. My reasoning is quoted as I wrote it, lightly edited for spelling.

**Score:** 5 of 10 exactly right (3, 4, 6, 7, 8), 3 partly (1, 5, 10: the right class was among my options), 2 wrong (2, 9).

## 2. Where my instinct diverged, and why

1. **I judged whether a use was good or harmful; the Act asks what it is used for and by whom.** On the stadium (2) I thought "safety, so fine"; the Act looks at biometric identification of everyone who enters and puts it in Annex III, and the GDPR blocks it harder than the AI Act does. The ban I half-remembered is scoped to law enforcement: the same actor-scoping that decided my own tool's class (Annex III points 6 and 7 apply to public authorities only).
2. **I mixed up security risk with risk class.** On the chatbot (3) and the translation tool (9) my worry was manipulation and data leakage. Those are real, but they are Week 9 and GDPR questions; the AI Act class depends on the use, and a translator with human review decides nothing about anyone.
3. **I reached for "prohibited" too fast on ranking people (1, 5).** The prohibited list is short and specific; ranking candidates or students is regulated as high-risk, not banned. What makes scenario 1 legally dangerous on top of that is the GDPR: rejecting 80% before any human looks.
4. **Where my personal view and the law differ (10), the law is explicit:** inferring ethnicity from faces is banned, whatever the commercial upside.

**What I take into my own work:** classify by use and by actor first, and keep security and data protection as separate checks. It is the same order my RIA follows.
