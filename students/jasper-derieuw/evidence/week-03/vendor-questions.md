## What came back

Nothing. As of Sunday 11 October 2026, I have received no reply to any of the three emails, and no automatic acknowledgement either. I confirmed this on that date.

Limits of what that silence shows:

- It does not show the vendors refuse to answer. Six days (email 1), four days (email 2) and two days (email 3) is short, and a press address may not be the right route for a product question.
- It does show that a student who states the question plainly, discloses who they are, and asks for one narrow fact cannot get that fact from either vendor within the first week. For a real buyer comparing vendors, that is a pattern worth noticing, not proof of anything.
- Taken with the 18 days of silence on the Week 1 pricing emails, it is consistent with a sales led model where answers come after a qualified conversation, not from an inbound email.

## Revised verdict

**Before:** Pilot, not buy.

**After: walk away for now.** Not for good.

What specifically changed it:

1. The pilot was only a reasonable step if the vendor could back up its headline claim. My Week 3 conclusion treated the 95% automation figure as something a pilot would test. I asked the one question that would tell me what the figure rests on, and got no answer. An unverifiable claim stays an Aspiration under FOMO question 3. It does not become a Fact because I was willing to test it.
2. The cost and fit questions (Q4 and Q5) are also unanswered, so the case for the full product over a simpler service is unchanged, and the true year one cost is still unknown. A pilot that starts without a cost ceiling is exactly the pattern the 80% rule warns about.
3. My own Build results point the same way. My prompt was wrong or inconsistent on 58.3% of inputs in Week 2. If the vendor cannot show me data for its figure, I should compare against my measured error rates, not the vendor's number.

"For now" is deliberate. If a reply arrives with real data, independently verified or not, the verdict should be reopened and the date of the change recorded here.

## What it changed

- **The verdict:** from Pilot, not buy to walk away for now, as above.
- **An assumption I dropped:** I had assumed that asking politely and honestly would be enough to get a straight answer to a narrow factual question. It was not, at least in the first week.
- **Something I now know:** the only trustworthy numbers in this project are the ones I measured myself (70.8% per run, 58.3% inconsistent). Any future vendor comparison should be made against those, and a vendor number only counts when its source is shown.

## What I would do next

- Check for replies on Friday 16 October 2026 and record the result here, including if it is still silence.
- Read the AJ Bell case study from the official Week 3 material and compare what it says about automation with the 95% claim.
- Try a general contact form instead of the press address, with the same single question.
- Not ask for a sales demo. A demo would answer a different question from the one I asked, and would move me into the sales process I am trying to evaluate from the outside.
