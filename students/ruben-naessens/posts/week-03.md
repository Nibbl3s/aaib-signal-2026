# AI in International Recruitment: Real Value or FOMO? Deconstructing HireVue’s AI Claims

*AI-assisted draft for Ruben’s review; the proposed analysis and verdict below have not yet been confirmed as his own conclusions.*

## The claim

Recruitment vendors sell relief from repetitive work. During my Worldwiders internship, I worked across marketing and sourcing; my Build now focuses on extracting qualifications from job advertisements. That experience makes efficiency claims relevant, but a faster workflow still needs accurate requirements.

HireVue’s [AI Hiring Agents page](https://www.hirevue.com/platform/ai-hiring-agents) advertises “60% Less time screening” and “90% Faster time to hire.” It also says its AI Interviewer “will combine science and voice.” These are vendor statements, not results from my Build. The [solutions page](https://www.hirevue.com/solutions) describes interviewing, assessment and recruitment workflows, a broader scope than vacancy extraction.

## The deconstruction

A useful distinction is between a product description, a measured outcome and a future promise. An impressive percentage does not establish what a buyer will achieve.

| Claim | Tag | Reasoning and evidence needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 60% less screening time | Avoidance | The headline presents a result without defining the screening task, comparison baseline, sample or review effort. Request the underlying measurement. |
| 90% faster hiring | Avoidance | “Faster” could refer to different calculations. Ask for before-and-after durations, the hiring stages included and the organisations measured. |
| The interviewer “will combine science and voice” | Aspiration | The future tense describes intended capability. Ask what is available now and what can be demonstrated. |

Avoidance here describes missing context in the headline presentation; it does not establish dishonesty or prove that supporting evidence does not exist elsewhere. A capability demonstrated in a product session would be checkable, but that demonstration alone would not verify a customer's claimed savings.

## The FOMO test

**What specific problem needs solving?** My Build addresses inconsistent qualification descriptions. Candidate screening and interviewing are different tasks. A screening-time headline cannot be transferred directly to reading vacancies. Before selecting a tool, I need a measured baseline and a target for that precise task.

**Can the claim be independently checked?** A useful pilot would use representative Dutch, French and English vacancies, with expected answers fixed beforehand. I would measure omitted requirements, invented mandatory qualifications and total review time. My original ten-ad baseline recorded one strict pass; it shows a weakness in my extraction prompt, not HireVue’s performance.

**Is there a simpler alternative?** A structured vacancy intake form could let hiring managers enter required and preferred qualifications directly. That option should be compared with AI extraction before investing in a broader platform.

## The verdict

The proposed decision is a conditional **pilot**, rather than a purchase based on headline percentages. First establish whether the product supports the actual task; if it does not, it is the wrong pilot candidate. If it does, request evidence from comparable customers and a complete cost estimate covering implementation, staff time, review and ongoing charges.

The pilot should demonstrate net time saved after correction while meeting an agreed quality threshold. Faster output that requires extensive checking is not yet a business benefit. No vendor response or independent HireVue test has been obtained for this post.
