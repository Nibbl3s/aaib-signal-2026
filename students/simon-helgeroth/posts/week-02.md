When AI is the Wrong Tool: A 5-Question Framework for Recruiters
---

Last time I wrote about the hidden costs of AI in recruitment screening, and I will now continue with other problems that may occur.  

AI in screening has different tasks and may consist of assessments, summaries, and recommendations on candidate applications. If or when, an AI makes mistakes in this particular area it is not only a missed candidate that is the risk. Depending on what cause the AI missorted the candidate it may be discrimination, and if it is it can lead to big expenses for the company. Both financial and reputational. Further, a candidate always has the right to know the reason for why he or she has been rejected, and how would it look if it was on false claims?  

To better evaluate an AI's capabilities in screening I will connect to a 5 question framework to see where it can be of assist or just the wrong option.  

### Question 1: Do you know the answer already?  
Well, of course not if you haven't looked at the application before getting a grade or assessment before having a chance to look at it.  

### Question 2: Is the cost of being wrong higher than the cost of being slow?  
This is one of the major dealbreakers in the use of AI in screening. As previously stated, the company's reputation is at risk. Being sued for discrimination creates high expenses in legal actions as well as it may make you lose clients.  

### Question 3: Is the information stable or changing rapidly?  
I would say that the information is stable as ads and resumes tend to look similar over time. It may however be a problem regarding phrasing. The AI may risk confusing requirements and a candidate's skills if it is not written similarly.  

### Question 4: Can you verify the AI's answer?  
Yes, you should be able to verify the AI's answer as long as it does not automatically reject a candidate.  

### Question 5: What's the simplest tool that solves it?  
The simplest tool to solve this problem is to not rely on AI. One way that recruiters try to find efficiency is to include questionnaires. Say for example that a driver's license is a requirement. When a candidate sends their application they need to answer yes or no questions regarding the requirements. This results in candidates being sorted with labels explaining whether they match the requirements or not.  

This makes room for the question of whether an AI can be helpful, for example in question 1. If you have the requirement questionnaire that candidates fill out, an AI would be able to reject them immediately. However, it also happens that requirements change over time and that clients make exceptions for candidates that would fit their culture. Another problem is also that AI learns from past data which may lead to biases in assessing resumes.  

So, what is the conclusion of AI in screening?  

My takeaway is that a short AI generated summary can be helpful but that it still may miss or hallucinate information. Therefore, I believe that a recruiter should be the one who assesses the applications, and in order to save time use questionnaires making decisions easier. Because is the supposed timesaving with AI (without even talking about the number of errors) worth it if it in case risks a biased algorithm or even lawsuit?
