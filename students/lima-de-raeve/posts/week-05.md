# Can AI Find Your Business? A GEO Audit of Tidio

## Introduction

For solo entrepreneurs, being visible online has always been essential. Traditionally, businesses invested in Search Engine Optimization (SEO) to appear higher in Google results and attract customers to their websites. However, with more people turning to AI assistants for recommendations, a new question arises: what happens when potential customers no longer browse search results but simply trust the companies recommended by AI?

This week, I explored Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) and investigated how discoverable an AI customer-service company actually is.

## The Company: Tidio

I selected Tidio, a customer-service platform offering live chat, chatbots, and Lyro, its AI-powered customer-service agent. Tidio is particularly relevant to my beat, *How AI Enables the One-Person Business*, because its tools can help small online retailers manage customer enquiries without hiring additional employees.

I had already investigated Tidio's vendor claims during Week 3, making it an interesting company to revisit from a different perspective: not whether its AI performs well, but whether potential customers can find it through AI recommendations.

## The Experiment: What Does AI Recommend?

I asked Perplexity the following question twice, in separate runs:

**"What are the best AI customer service platforms for a solo entrepreneur running a small online clothing store in Belgium?"**

Both responses recommended the same five companies: Tidio, Gorgias, Crisp, Help Scout, and Freshdesk.

Interestingly, Tidio appeared first in both responses, while the ranking of other companies changed. Pricing details and explanations also differed.

This demonstrated that AI recommendations can be consistent in company selection while varying in presentation. Unlike traditional Google rankings, AI-generated recommendations do not necessarily follow a fixed order.

## The GEO Audit

Using the six-criterion framework from class, I evaluated Tidio's AI discoverability.

| GEO Criterion | Score |
|---|---|
| Structured Data & Schema Markup | 2/3 |
| Content Parseability | 3/3 |
| Third-Party Mention Density | 3/3 |
| AI Citation Presence | 2/3 |
| Authority & Expertise Signals | 2/3 |
| Brand Direct-Search Strength | 3/3 |
| **Total** | **15/18** |

Tidio's website uses Schema.org and JSON-LD structured data, which I verified by inspecting its source code. Its product pages also provide clear descriptions and structured information about its services.

Furthermore, Tidio has a strong third-party presence through platforms such as G2, Capterra, and Shopify. Its customer stories and educational resources provide additional credibility.

Its AI citation score remains provisional because my experiment only covered two Perplexity runs using the same question.

## The Verdict

With a score of **15/18**, Tidio falls into the **AI-Present** category according to our course framework.

Its strong online presence and clear positioning make it discoverable for relevant AI customer-service queries. However, this does not guarantee that other AI assistants or different prompts will recommend it.

I would recommend that Tidio continue improving its structured data and test its visibility across multiple AI platforms and customer questions.

## The Insight

My biggest takeaway is that AI is changing the way businesses compete for visibility. Traditional SEO focuses on attracting website visits, while GEO focuses on being included in AI-generated answers.

For solo entrepreneurs, this creates both opportunities and risks. A small business might gain visibility without an enormous advertising budget, but it could also be overlooked entirely if AI assistants consistently recommend competitors.

**My question for next week:** Will GEO make it easier for small businesses to compete with established brands, or will AI recommendations make the biggest companies even more dominant?
