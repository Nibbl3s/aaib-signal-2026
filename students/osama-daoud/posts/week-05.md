When a head of performance asks an AI assistant "which tool can predict injuries in my squad?", the answer is a shortlist of three to six names. A vendor that is not on it is not rejected. It is simply never considered. I audited Zone7 to see if it makes that list.

## The company

Zone7 sells AI injury-risk forecasting to professional teams: it reads a club's training and match data and flags players with a higher risk of getting injured. It raised about $10 million and worked with clubs in football, the NFL and the NBA. In 2024 it became part of svexa, a company for training and performance data. I chose it because it is the vendor in my capstone question: should Club Brugge pilot Zone7?

The scorecard looks contradictory. Zone7 does almost nothing to help machines read its own site: no prices, no FAQ, no structured data at all, and a brand name that Google confuses with gardening. Yet ChatGPT put it first, above Catapult and Kitman Labs, in both runs. The reason is the third row: years of press coverage that describes Zone7 as "the injury prediction company". The AI repeats what others wrote, not what Zone7 wrote. One warning: a web search I ran with Claude on the same question did not name Zone7 at all, so the result depends on the engine.

## The verdict

Would an AI agent recommend Zone7? Yes, today ChatGPT does, and as its top pick. That makes it AI-Present. But both answers came with a warning: "Forecasts need to be tested against the club's own injury records. An alert is not a diagnosis." ChatGPT then told the buyer to ask every vendor about false alarms.

That is Zone7's biggest gap. Its own validation study reports the injuries it caught, not its false alarms. So the AI recommends Zone7 and, in the same answer, tells the club to ask for the one number Zone7 does not publish. The second gap is fragility: the coverage stops around 2023. If new press goes to competitors like VALITICA, the shortlist can change.

## The insight

In my beat, AI discoverability comes from what journalists write, not from the vendor's website. That is good news for vendors with old press and bad news for new ones. Clubs buy through demos and referrals, so Zone7's business risk is low. But AI assistants are now writing the buyer's checklist too, and that checklist asks for independent validation and false alarms. For sports-tech vendors, being named is no longer enough; being able to answer the AI's questions is next.

## The question

If an AI tells Club Brugge to ask for a false-alarm rate, what happens when Zone7 cannot give one?
