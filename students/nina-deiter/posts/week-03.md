# AI in Creative Agencies: Real Value or FOMO? Deconstructing monday.com's AI Claims

**Beat:** When does AI actually help project managers in creative agencies — and when does it get in the way?

### 1. The Hook

Creative agencies constantly balance deadlines, client feedback, changing requirements and creative freedom. AI promises to reduce administrative work, but does automating project management actually improve creative outcomes, or does it introduce new problems?

### 2. The Claim

monday.com is a work management platform that increasingly integrates AI into project planning, monitoring and reporting. Its AI capabilities include automated project plans, AI agents, risk detection and status reporting.

The company makes several interesting claims:

**Claim 1:** “Plan any project in minutes with AI-generated project plans.”

According to monday.com, AI can turn a project description into tasks, milestones, dependencies and assigned responsibilities.

**Claim 2:** “Flag project risks and delays before they impact delivery.”

Its AI-powered risk analysis reportedly monitors timelines, workloads and dependencies to identify potential problems early.

**Claim 3:** monday.com reports that customers have reduced project planning time by half and saved over 98 hours of manual work through AI-powered workflows.

For creative agencies, these promises sound attractive. Project managers often spend considerable time coordinating designers, copywriters, clients and deadlines. However, saving administrative time does not necessarily mean producing better creative work.

### 3. The Deconstruction

| Claim | Tag | Analysis |
|---|---|---|
| AI-generated project plans in minutes | Aspiration | The functionality is plausible and testable, but generating a plan quickly does not prove its quality. Creative projects often change after client feedback, making initial plans unreliable. |
| AI identifies risks before delays occur | Aspiration | This depends on accurate project data. AI may detect overdue tasks or workload conflicts, but can it anticipate subjective creative feedback or unexpected client revisions? No independent accuracy figures are provided. |
| Planning time reduced by 50%; 98+ hours saved | Aspiration | These are vendor-reported customer outcomes. The website does not establish that these results are typical for creative agencies or attributable solely to AI. |
| AI-credit consumption and pricing | Fact | monday.com documents its credit system, including 120 credits per hour for AI Notetaker and variable costs for AI agents. These figures are published and checkable. |
| Impact on creative quality | Avoidance | The marketing focuses on efficiency but provides no clear evidence that AI improves creative quality, client satisfaction or the number of revisions. |

The biggest issue is the difference between **measurable efficiency and actual project success**.

Automating status updates is relatively straightforward. Evaluating whether a campaign concept is original, whether client feedback has been understood correctly, or whether a designer needs more creative freedom is much harder.

There is also a risk that AI-generated plans create false confidence. A structured timeline may look convincing while overlooking the unpredictable nature of creative work.

### 4. The FOMO Test

**Q1 – What specific problem does AI solve?**

For creative agencies, reducing repetitive reporting, task assignment and administrative coordination is a legitimate goal. However, agencies should first measure how much time project managers currently spend on these activities. Without a baseline, productivity improvements remain assumptions.

**Q3 – Can the claims be verified?**

An agency could test monday.com's AI on actual client projects, comparing planning time, reporting accuracy, missed deadlines and revision cycles against its current process. Importantly, project managers should also evaluate whether AI suggestions are useful or require additional correction.

**Q5 – Is there a simpler solution?**

Traditional monday.com boards, templates and rule-based automations might already solve many coordination problems without generative AI. A comparison is necessary to determine whether AI provides additional value beyond regular project management software.

### 5. The Verdict: Pilot, Don't Buy Blindly

monday.com's AI appears promising for administrative project management tasks, particularly reporting, task coordination and identifying potential scheduling conflicts.

However, its marketing does not sufficiently demonstrate improvements in creative quality or client outcomes.

I would recommend a limited pilot comparing AI-assisted workflows with existing processes, including traditional automation. The decision should depend on measurable time savings, accuracy, total costs and feedback from creative teams.

**My conclusion for my beat:** AI helps creative project managers when it removes repetitive coordination work and creates more time for human creativity. It gets in the way when unreliable suggestions, excessive automation or additional checking reduce flexibility and distract from the creative process.

The objective should not be to automate creativity, but to give people more time for it.
