# Signal Post 5 – When AI Becomes the Gatekeeper

**Beat:** When does AI actually help project managers in creative agencies — and when does it get in the way?

## Hook
What happens when potential customers stop searching through Google and instead ask an AI assistant to recommend a company? 
Even a well-established agency might suddenly become less visible, depending on which AI tool the customer uses.

## The Company
For this week's GEO audit, I chose **Mutabor**, a German design and branding agency that I also work for. 
Mutabor develops holistic brand solutions, ranging from brand strategy and corporate design to packaging and brand experiences.
I wanted to understand whether a well-established agency with recognizable clients and strong industry recognition would automatically be recommended by AI assistants when potential customers search for a suitable design partner.

## The Audit
I evaluated Mutabor using the six GEO criteria and gave it an overall score of **14/18 (AI-Present)**.
The website performs relatively well in structured data (2/3) and content parseability (2/3). It uses valid JSON-LD markup and presents its services through clearly organized categories. However, specific Service schemas are missing from the tested homepage, and some descriptions could be clearer.
Mutabor also benefits from third-party mentions (2/3) and authority signals (3/3). Industry publications and design awards, including Red Dot, provide independent evidence of its expertise.

The most interesting results came from testing **AI Citation Presence (2/3)**. I asked different AI assistants to recommend an experienced international design agency capable of developing a brand holistically.
ChatGPT recommended Mutabor first, while Perplexity's results depended heavily on the language and wording of the prompt, Langdock for example did not recommend Mutabor initially.

Mutabor also scored strongly in brand direct-search (3/3), although finding the agency using generic service-related keywords was more difficult.

## The Verdict
Overall, Mutabor is relatively visible in AI-generated recommendations, but this visibility is inconsistent.
In the vulnerability assessment, I placed Mutabor in the **low-risk quadrant**. Its business model depends less on website traffic and more on long-term client relationships, referrals and complex creative services that AI cannot easily replace completely.
However, this does not mean GEO is irrelevant. Even if AI cannot replace the agency's services, it can influence which agencies potential customers consider in the first place.
A limitation of my audit is that I only tested a small number of prompts across different AI tools. Therefore, the results demonstrate possible visibility differences rather than a reliable ranking.

## The Insight

My biggest takeaway is that **being a well-known company does not automatically guarantee visibility in AI searches**.
The results also showed me that GEO is not simply about improving a website's technical structure. It is about understanding how AI systems interpret a company's expertise and decide which providers to recommend.
I also realized that AI visibility and business vulnerability are two different things. Mutabor may be relatively protected from losing website traffic, but it could still miss opportunities if AI assistants recommend competitors instead.

## The Question
As AI increasingly influences how customers discover businesses, should companies actively optimize their visibility in AI recommendations, even if traditional search traffic is not their main source of customers?
