# Skills Passport — Lena Devos *(example)*

Thirteen skills. Three checkpoints: end of Weeks 4, 8 and 12.
Rating: 🟢 Confident / 🟡 Getting there / 🔴 Need practice — plus **one sentence of evidence**
(something you did, not how you feel).

This is not a grade. Nobody marks it. Your teacher reads it and tells you where they see it
differently — the disagreement is the entire point. Commit it **on its own**, not bundled
with a post. In Week 12 you will open this file's history and see three versions of yourself.

## Checkpoint 1 — end of Week 4

| # | Skill | Rating | Evidence |
|---|-------|--------|----------|
| 1 | Cost literacy | 🟢 | I understood why the cheapest AI model is not always the best choice economically. For example, using AI in German can require between 24.7% and 96.3% more tokens than in English, depending on the input/output. Furthermore, token costs are not the only factor to consider. Error correction and the resulting human workload can outweigh the savings from choosing a cheaper model.|
| 2 | Capability skepticism | 🟡 | I implemented my Feedback-to-Task Assistant for project managers in creative agencies. The tests showed me that AI can produce good and reliable results, but it can also fail when dealing with ambiguous feedback where the required action is unclear (e.g., test case T10). I would still like to investigate how the tool performs with different types of feedback. |
| 3 | Vendor claim detection | 🟡 | I learned that AI should not automatically be applied to every process. Instead, we should first ask ourselves whether AI is actually necessary. I also realized that vendor claims should not always be taken at face value. In the NexusAI analysis, I evaluated 16 claims and classified them as Fact, Aspiration or Avoidance. However, I still need more practice distinguishing between these categories confidently. |
| 4 | Value quantification | 🟡 | Analyzing the cost structure of my own tool was particularly interesting and helped me reinforce my knowledge from Week 1. My business case showed a potential annual net benefit of €5,325 from using AI to process client feedback. The calculation remained positive even when I reduced the estimated time savings by 50%. However, my assumptions were mainly based on my own experience working as a project manager in a creative agency rather than independently validated data, which is still a weakness of my analysis. |
