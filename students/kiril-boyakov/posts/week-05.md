My family works in logistics, so I am interested in how freight businesses get chosen as well as how they calculate prices. If buyers ask AI to recommend a platform, the shortlist may be formed before they visit a website. My audit of Freightos suggests that visibility matters, but appearing in an answer does not establish suitability for a shipment.

Freightos provides freight pricing, booking and market information. It fits my beat because comparing rates is one step towards preparing a freight quote. I selected the company before testing, rather than pretending an AI answer led me to it. The audit used public websites and agent-operated browser tests on 25 October 2026.

The six-criterion score was **12/18**, within the course's “AI-Present” band:

|---|---:|---|
| Structured data | 1 | Organization and FAQ markup; no Product or Service type in the two inspected pages' JSON-LD. |
| Content parseability | 2 | Clear audience sections, service headings and questions. |

The [evidence file](../week-05-evidence/analysis.md) contains quotations, sources and scoring decisions. The total is a course assessment, not a guarantee that every assistant will recommend Freightos.

Two fresh Perplexity searches used the same question about ocean-freight platforms for a Belgian importer. Both named Freightos first and SeaRates second. However, both gave a generic digital-forwarder category instead of a third company. A stable answer was still incomplete, and Belgium-specific route coverage remained unverified.

Perplexity then required sign-up. The remaining variations used Google AI Overviews, so this was not a controlled four-run comparison of one model. Google's forwarder-software answer placed Freightos third; its benchmarking answer named the Freightos Baltic Index. Freightos appeared in all four successful outputs, but that small mixed-engine sample cannot establish a general recommendation rate.

One useful omission was iContainers, which did not appear in the repeated importer answers. Its [website](https://www.icontainers.com/) describes a digital freight forwarder with online quotation and booking. It appeared in Google's benchmarking answer instead. Relevant public content therefore does not guarantee inclusion in every shortlist.

My vulnerability assessment is moderate overall. AI can replace some visits to explanatory pages and influence which intermediary buyers choose. However, Freightos also supports operational workflows. Summarising shipping information does not itself confirm capacity, process a booking or fulfil a shipment. Agents connected to those services could change how customers access them.

My priority improvement is clearer machine-readable service information identifying who each product serves. The business should measure accurate recommendations and qualified bookings, not just mentions. A hypothetical 60% traffic decline cannot be translated directly into lost revenue without knowing which customers and transactions depend on that traffic.

If AI becomes the buyer's first contact, what would make a freight platform remain the place where the shipment is actually booked?
