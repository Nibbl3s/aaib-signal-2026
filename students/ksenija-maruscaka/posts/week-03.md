# Marketing AI: Real Value or FOMO? Deconstructing Phrasee’s Generative Copy Claims

**1. The Hook**
In marketing, everybody wants personalization at scale, but nobody wants to calculate the unit costs of generative AI to get there. Brands are rushing to buy "AI copywriters" out of fear of falling behind, but the gap between a marketing pitch and actual ROI is where budgets go to die.

**2. The Claim**
Let’s look at Phrasee, a popular AI platform tailored for enterprise marketing. Phrasee claims to be the "world’s most advanced generative AI for marketing." Their core pitch includes several strong statements: 
*   "AI-generated copy that outperforms human-written copy 71% of the time."
*   "Trained on millions of successful data points."
*   "Safely generates 100% on-brand content."

**3. The Deconstruction**
When we pull these claims apart using a Skeptic’s tagging framework, the reality becomes a bit murkier:

| Claim | Tag | Reasoning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| "Outperforms human copy 71% of the time" | Aspiration / Avoidance | Who are the humans? Are they comparing the AI to an award-winning copywriter or an intern? Furthermore, what defines "outperforms"—open rates, click-throughs, or actual closed sales? |
| "Trained on millions of successful data points" | Avoidance | While likely a Fact that it was trained on vast data, it avoids stating *whose* data. Generic marketing data doesn't necessarily translate to high conversion rates for *our* specific niche audience. |
| "100% on-brand content safely" | Aspiration | "100%" is a massive red flag in GenAI. LLMs hallucinate by design. Guaranteeing 100% brand safety is an aspiration; human oversight is still absolutely required. |

**4. The FOMO Test**
Let's run this through the strategic fit framework:
*   **Q1: What specific problem does this solve?** It solves the bottleneck of A/B testing copy at scale. This is a real, valid problem for high-volume email and ad marketers.
*   **Q3: Can we verify the claims?** Yes, but only if they allow a pilot. We would need to run Phrasee's copy against our own historical control copy for 30 days to see if the "71% outperformance" holds true for our audience.
*   **Q4: What's the total cost?** Beyond the license, the real cost here is *human editing time*. If the AI generates copy but a senior marketer has to spend hours reviewing it for brand safety, the total cost of ownership skyrockets.

**5. The Verdict: PILOT (With strict conditions)**
Unlike vague "supply chain AI," generative text for marketing is a proven modality (Week 2). However, the 71% claim is purely aspirational until proven on our specific consumer base. My verdict is to **pilot** the software, but only if the vendor allows a 30-day A/B test on our own audience, measuring against actual conversions (not just click rates), and with a clear understanding of the human-in-the-loop editing costs.
