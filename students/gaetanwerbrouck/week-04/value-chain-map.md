# Week 4 — TechCorp Value Chain Map

## AI potential by activity

| Activity | Type | AI potential | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product Development | Primary | Yes | AI can draft tests, summarize code changes and suggest documentation, while developers still review the output before release. |
| Marketing & Sales | Primary | Yes | AI can draft campaign variations and first-pass proposals from approved CRM data, with staff checking facts and personalization. |
| Service Delivery | Primary | Maybe | AI could help explain service incidents or spot usage patterns, but core cloud hosting and predictable operational tasks may be better handled by conventional monitoring and automation. |
| Customer Support | Primary | Yes | 30% of 4,200 monthly tickets are repetitive, so AI triage and knowledge-base suggestions could reduce searching and route tickets faster. |
| Customer Success | Primary | Maybe | Pattern analysis could flag accounts at risk of churn, but account managers must validate the signal and handle retention conversations. |
| IT & Security | Support | Maybe | AI could summarize helpdesk requests and prioritize security alerts, but false negatives and false positives need human escalation. |
| Finance | Support | Yes | Invoice extraction can reduce manual entry and identify fields that do not match the source invoice or SAP record. |
| HR | Support | Maybe | AI can answer routine policy questions and organize applicant information, but it should not make final hiring decisions or rank candidates without fairness controls. |
| Legal & Compliance | Support | Maybe | AI can highlight unusual clauses and prepare a first-pass comparison, while a qualified lawyer verifies the meaning and decides what to do. |
| Data & Analytics | Support | Yes | AI can draft SQL and recurring report summaries, provided an analyst validates queries, totals and conclusions against trusted data. |
| Operations | Support | Maybe | AI may summarize contracts and reports, but simple renewal alerts are more reliably handled by calendar or rules-based automation. |

## Three activities with the strongest initial business case

1. **Finance — invoice processing.** The company processes 600 invoices per month, spends 150 hours per month entering data, and has a 12% manual-entry error rate. Invoice fields can be checked against the original invoice and SAP, making the process measurable and reviewable.
2. **Customer Support — ticket triage.** The team receives 4,200 tickets per month and 30% are repetitive. AI can classify tickets and suggest relevant knowledge-base articles, while agents remain responsible for answers and escalations.
3. **Data & Analytics — recurring reports.** Fifty recurring reports take about two hours each to build and rerun every month. AI could draft SQL or report summaries, but analysts must compare outputs with trusted queries and source totals before sharing them.

These are candidate areas, not automatic approval for AI. A spreadsheet, workflow rule or existing software feature should be compared wherever it can solve the problem more simply.
