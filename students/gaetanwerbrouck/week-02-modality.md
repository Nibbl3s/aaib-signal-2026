# Week 2: Modality reasoning

**Beat:** How Football Clubs Use Data to Find Undervalued Players

## Borderline case 1: Fraud detection

**Classification: AI-Risky**

Fraud detection can involve finding unusual patterns in large amounts of data, which is a task AI may help with. However, a false positive can wrongly flag a legitimate transaction, while a false negative can allow fraud to pass. The system also needs reliable, representative data and a clear way for a human to review uncertain cases.

For football scouting, the comparison is a model flagging an unusual performance pattern. A flag should be treated as a lead for investigation, not proof that a player is good or bad.

## Borderline case 2: Hiring screening

**Classification: AI-Risky**

AI can sort applications or extract information, but screening people can affect important opportunities. The system may miss relevant experience, reproduce patterns in past decisions, or treat wording and background as signals that are not actually relevant to the job. A human should define the criteria, check the output, and remain responsible for decisions.

The football equivalent is screening players based on a narrow set of statistics. A player who does not fit the data pattern may still have qualities the model does not measure.

## Borderline case 3: Legal contract review

**Classification: AI-Risky**

AI can summarize clauses and point out text that may deserve attention. But a confident-sounding summary can omit a condition or misunderstand how clauses interact. Because a missed issue can have serious consequences, the output needs to be checked against the original contract by someone qualified.

A scouting system faces a similar limitation when interpreting contract details such as expiry dates or release clauses. It can extract what is written, but should not invent missing information or make a final decision from incomplete context.

## Overall reflection

The important distinction is not simply whether AI can produce an answer. It is whether the answer can be checked, how much harm an error could cause, and whether a simpler tool would do the job. In my beat, AI may help organize information and identify candidates for review. It should not be treated as proof that a player is undervalued, because that conclusion depends on context and human judgment.
