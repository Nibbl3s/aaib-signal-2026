AI Costs in Business Management
1. Introduction
In business management, companies are increasingly using AI for tasks such as customer service, communication and analysing information. However, it is important to understand that AI is not free, especially when it is used thousands of times per month. Understanding the cost per token and the impact of language can help a company make better decisions about how and where to use AI.
2. The Numbers
Imagine a Belgian company that uses an AI chatbot for customer service. The chatbot handles 30,000 customer emails per month. Each email contains around 200 input tokens, and the AI generates around 400 output tokens per response.
This means that each customer interaction uses:
•	200 input tokens
•	400 output tokens
•	600 tokens in total
For 30,000 emails, this gives:
Input:
30,000 × 200 = 6,000,000 input tokens
Output:
30,000 × 400 = 12,000,000 output tokens
Using the course pricing of $0.03 per 1,000 tokens, the total would be:
Input cost:
6,000,000 ÷ 1,000 × $0.03 = $180
Output cost:
12,000,000 ÷ 1,000 × $0.03 = $360
Total monthly AI cost:
$180 + $360 = $540
Using the course exchange rate of $1 = €0.92, this is approximately €496.80 per month.
This calculation also shows why the language of the customer matters. English uses around 133 tokens for 100 words, while Dutch uses around 186 tokens and French around 169 tokens. This means that the same business communication can require more tokens in another language and therefore increase the AI cost.
3. The Insight
What surprised me most was how the cost of AI is actually calculated. Before this lesson, I did not know what tokens were or that they were used to determine the price of AI. I expected the cost to depend mainly on the number of questions or messages, but I learned that the amount of text processed by the AI is also important.
Another thing that surprised me was the effect of language. I did not know that an AI response in French or Dutch could cost more than the same response in English. For example, the course shows that Dutch requires around 40% more tokens than English for equivalent business text.
The most important conclusion for me is that companies should not only look at whether AI works well. They should also consider how many times it is used, how many tokens it processes and which languages are involved. A cost that seems small for one customer can become significant when thousands of customers use the system every month. AI costs are therefore not only about the price per response, but also about the scale at which a company uses it.
