# Signal Post 4 — Show Me the Numbers: AI in Fashion Marketing & E-commerce

AI is often presented as an easy way for fashion retailers to create more content, faster. But producing more product descriptions is not automatically valuable. The real question is whether the time saved is worth more than the technology, human supervision and errors cost.

## The Candidate: AI-Generated Product Descriptions

For this value audit, I use Omoda as the company context and examine AI-generated fashion product descriptions. Omoda sells shoes, clothing and accessories. Product descriptions are part of the e-commerce value chain: they support product presentation and help customers understand what they are buying. The proposed tool generates descriptions from supplied product information, such as colour, material, fit and available sizes. Employees would review each description before publication.

The company-specific figures below are hypothetical assumptions, not verified Omoda data. My own Build was tested on 12 inputs: 8 outputs were fully successful (66.7%) and 4 were unsuccessful (33.3%). Problems included unsupported marketing claims, missing information and contradictory sizing details.

## The Numbers: What Could the Value Be?

I assume a retailer processes 5,000 descriptions per month, or 60,000 per year. Writing one description manually takes an estimated five minutes. With AI, an employee spends two minutes reviewing each output. The loaded hourly cost of a marketing specialist is estimated at €42.

| Benefit category | Annual value | Calculation or evidence |
|---|---:|---|
| Time saved | €126,000 | 60,000 × (5 − 2) minutes ÷ 60 × €42/hour |
| Revenue increase | €0 | No verified evidence of additional sales |
| Cost reduction | €0 | No separate direct cost saving verified |
| Risk mitigation | €0 | No reliable probability and impact data |
| **Gross quantified benefit** | **€126,000** | Before correction and AI costs |

The €126,000 is the gross value of three minutes saved per description, before accounting for failed outputs. Applying my preliminary 33.3% failure rate to the annual volume gives approximately 20,000 unsuccessful outputs. If each requires five extra minutes of correction, the annual correction cost is 20,000 × 5 ÷ 60 × €42 = €70,000. This correction time is an assumption that needs validation with a product-content practitioner.

AI usage costs are relatively small. At an estimated 150 input tokens and 250 output tokens per description, annual usage is 9 million input tokens and 15 million output tokens. Using the course reference prices of $2 per million input tokens and $12 per million output tokens, the annual token cost is ($18 + $180) × 0.92 = approximately €182.

Other Year 1 costs are estimated at €10,000 for implementation, €2,000 for integration and €2,000 for annual maintenance. Adding these to token costs and corrections gives total Year 1 AI costs of €84,182.

The estimated Year 1 net benefit is €126,000 − €84,182 = **€41,818**. ROI is €41,818 ÷ €84,182 × 100 = **49.7%**. For payback, the one-time implementation and integration costs (€12,000) are divided by the monthly recurring net benefit. After corrections, tokens and maintenance, the recurring annual benefit is €53,818, or about €4,485 per month. This gives an estimated payback period of **2.7 months**.

## The Pressure Test: Where Could the Case Break?

What if the time-saving benefit is 50% lower? Gross benefit would fall from €126,000 to €63,000. If the other cost assumptions remain unchanged, the Year 1 net benefit becomes €63,000 − €84,182 = **−€21,182**. The business case would then be negative.

There is also a measurement risk: 66.7% success is based on only 12 test inputs, too small a sample to predict performance at Omoda. Monthly volume, correction time and the five-minute manual baseline are also assumptions. If failed outputs take longer to repair, or the real volume is lower, ROI could shrink further.

## The Insight: ROI or FOMO?

The biggest lesson is that low token prices do not automatically make an AI project valuable. Here, token costs are only about €182 per year, while human correction is estimated at €70,000. The main financial question is not which model is cheapest, but whether the tool produces reliable descriptions that genuinely reduce employee time.

There is potential, but the current evidence does not justify a full rollout. I recommend a controlled pilot with a larger product sample. Measure writing, review and correction time, as well as factual accuracy, then replace assumptions with practitioner data. That is the difference between investing because AI is fashionable and investing because the numbers support it.
