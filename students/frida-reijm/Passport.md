## Checkpoint 1 — end of Week 4

| # | Skill | Rating | Evidence |
|---|-------|--------|----------|
| 1 | Cost literacy | 🟡 | In Week 1, I compared AI model pricing and calculated token costs for different usage levels. In my Week 4 Omoda business case, I also calculated estimated annual token costs, implementation costs and maintenance costs. I understand the calculations better now, but I still need to improve at checking all assumptions behind them. |
| 2 | Capability skepticism | 🟡 | I tested my AI tool for generating fashion product descriptions with 12 inputs. Eight outputs were fully successful, while four contained issues such as unsupported marketing claims, missing information or contradictory sizing details. This showed me that an AI output can sound convincing while still being incorrect, so human review is important. |
| 3 | Vendor claim detection | 🟡 | In Week 3, I analysed claims about AI in fashion marketing and e-commerce, including claims connected to Omoda's Discover the Look feature. I learned to question how results such as higher conversion rates are measured and whether there is enough evidence to support a claim. I still need more practice finding independent evidence to verify vendor or company claims. |
| 4 | Value quantification | 🟡 | In Week 4, I created a business case for AI-generated product descriptions in a hypothetical Omoda scenario. I estimated a gross annual time-saving benefit of €126,000 and, after estimated correction and implementation costs, a Year 1 net benefit of €41,818. I also tested what would happen if the time-saving benefit was 50% lower. The figures are assumptions that still need validation with real practitioner data. |
