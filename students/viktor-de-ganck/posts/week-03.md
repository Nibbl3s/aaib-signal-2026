Every AI finance tool now promises to do what I want my research desk to do: pull scattered market data together and explain it. Before trusting one with my money, I wanted to know which of its claims I can actually check.

## The claim

Perplexity, the AI search company, markets its finance features to people like me. Its use-case page for comparing stocks promises that "Perplexity can instantly aggregate real-time stock data" and gives "accurate, trusted, and real-time answers to any question". A company blog post from 25 August 2026 goes further: "Every figure traces to the source record it came from", with filings "sourced directly from SEC EDGAR XBRL filings within minutes of publication". The same post says these licensed data connectors come "with an eligible partner license" and that "there's no separate data contract with Perplexity". Neither page carries a disclaimer about accuracy or investment advice.

## Deconstruction

My test for every claim: could I verify it with one follow-up question?

| Claim | Tag | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "instantly aggregate real-time stock data" | Avoidance | Real-time from where? No data source is named, and "real-time" can mean live or 15 minutes delayed. |
| "accurate, trusted, and real-time answers to any question" | Aspiration | A promise about every possible question can never be disproven. |
| "Every figure traces to the source record" | Fact, with a catch | Checkable: click a figure and see its source. But it is promised for the licensed connectors, not for the free stock pages. |
| "from SEC EDGAR XBRL filings within minutes" | Fact | A named source and a time window: I can compare the timestamp with the filing. |
| "eligible partner license" / "no separate data contract with Perplexity" | Avoidance | Literally true, but it hides who you do pay: the data provider. |

The pattern: the checkable claims belong to the paid, professional product. The page aimed at retail investors gets the vague ones. What is sold is traceable data; what a retail investor is promised is speed and "accuracy" without a named source.

## The FOMO test

**What problem does it solve?** A real one: my sources are scattered. That passes.

**Can I verify the claims?** Partly, which is more than most vendors offer. But in my own Build, a much simpler task, classifying news items with clear rules, the AI was fully right on only 9 of 15 items. An answer with a citation can still misread its source.

**What is the total cost?** The subscription is only part of it. Traceable data needs a partner license: the same lesson as my first post, where the AI was cheap and the data was not. Add the time to check figures before acting on them.

## Verdict

Pilot, not buy. Perplexity is ahead of most tools because some of its claims can be checked at all, which also makes a pilot easy. For one month I would use it next to my usual sources and check ten figures against their original documents before trusting it with a trade. If they hold up, it earns a place on my research desk. Until then, its most valuable feature is the one it advertises least: the link back to the source, which I still have to click.

## The question

If a tool shows you its sources, how often do you actually click them?
